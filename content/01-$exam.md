# Chapter 1 Exam Questions: Chemical Foundations of Biochemistry

These multiple-choice questions are designed for upper-level Bloom's taxonomy work in Biochemistry I and are aligned with the Chapter 1 worksheet activities on thermodynamics, equilibrium, kinetics, catalysis, functional groups, water chemistry, acids and bases, and buffers [file:111][file:122]. The question set emphasizes application, analysis, and evaluation rather than simple recall, which is consistent with effective flipped biochemistry course design [web:6][web:22][web:78].

## Questions

1. A reaction in a cell has a positive delta G under the measured intracellular conditions, but the same reaction has a negative delta G degree prime. Which explanation best accounts for this difference?
	A. The intracellular reactant and product concentrations shift the actual free energy change away from the standard biochemical state.
	B. The enzyme in the cell increases the activation energy and reverses the sign of the biochemical standard free energy.
	C. The reaction becomes nonbiological in the cell because standard biochemical conditions apply only outside organisms.
	D. The equilibrium constant changes whenever the reaction is measured in a cytosolic rather than aqueous environment.

2. A student argues that a reaction with delta G = -30 kJ/mol must proceed rapidly in a cell. Which response best evaluates this claim?

A. The claim is correct because a strongly negative free energy change always predicts a high reaction rate in vivo.
B. The claim is incomplete because thermodynamic favorability does not indicate whether the activation barrier is low.
C. The claim is false because all exergonic reactions are prevented by intracellular homeostatic regulation mechanisms.
D. The claim is irrelevant because delta G only applies to inorganic reactions and not to biochemical pathways.

3. An enzyme lowers the activation energy of a reaction but does not alter the energies of reactants or products. Which outcome is most consistent with that mechanism?

A. The reaction reaches equilibrium more quickly, but the equilibrium position remains unchanged.
B. The reaction forms more product at equilibrium because the enzyme increases the product free energy level.
C. The reaction becomes favorable only in the presence of the enzyme because catalysts redefine spontaneity.
D. The reaction consumes less substrate overall because the catalyst lowers the enthalpy of the system.

4. A metabolic reaction has delta G degree prime = +14 kJ/mol. It is coupled to ATP hydrolysis with delta G degree prime = -30.5 kJ/mol. What is the approximate net delta G degree prime for the coupled process?

A. -44.5 kJ/mol.
B. -16.5 kJ/mol.
C. +16.5 kJ/mol.
D. +44.5 kJ/mol.

5. A reaction follows the rate law: rate = k[A]. If the concentration of A is reduced from 8 mM to 2 mM while temperature and k remain constant, what is the best prediction?

A. The rate decreases to one-fourth of its original value.
B. The rate decreases to one-half of its original value.
C. The rate increases fourfold because the reaction shifts toward product formation.
D. The rate remains unchanged because k already accounts for the reactant concentration.

6. A biochemical process shows delta H less than 0 and delta S less than 0. Which conclusion is most defensible?

A. The process will be spontaneous at all temperatures because both enthalpy and entropy changes are favorable.
B. The process will be spontaneous only at low temperatures where the enthalpy term dominates the Gibbs equation.
C. The process will be spontaneous only at high temperatures where the entropy term becomes increasingly favorable.
D. The process can never be spontaneous because negative entropy change always prevents biochemical reactions.

7. A buffer contains 0.20 M HA and 0.020 M A-. Which statement best describes the relationship between pH and pKa for this solution?

A. The pH is one unit above the pKa because the protonated form is present in excess.
B. The pH is one unit below the pKa because the protonated form is tenfold more abundant.
C. The pH is equal to the pKa because all effective buffers contain matched acid and base concentrations.
D. The pH is unrelated to the pKa because buffer calculations depend only on the total solute concentration.

8. A weak acid has a pKa of 6.5. Which situation would produce the greatest fraction of the deprotonated form?

A. A solution at pH 5.5 with strong hydrogen-bonding interactions to water.
B. A solution at pH 6.5 with equal concentrations of acid and conjugate base.
C. A solution at pH 7.5 with all other conditions held approximately constant.
D. A solution at pH 6.0 with a high dielectric constant and fixed ionic strength.

9. A researcher adds a small amount of strong acid to two solutions: Solution 1 is pure water, and Solution 2 is a buffer containing similar total solute concentration. Which comparison is most accurate?

A. Solution 1 shows a smaller pH change because water has a higher dielectric constant than the buffer.
B. Solution 2 shows a smaller pH change because the conjugate base can absorb added protons.
C. Solution 1 shows no pH change because water already contains both hydrogen and hydroxide ions.
D. Solution 2 shows a larger pH change because the added acid disrupts the equilibrium of both buffer components.

10. A drug candidate contains a long hydrocarbon chain and one hydroxyl group. Which prediction is most justified regarding its aqueous behavior?

A. It will be highly soluble because a single hydroxyl group dominates the contribution of the hydrocarbon region.
B. It will be poorly soluble because the large nonpolar region outweighs the effect of one small polar group.
C. It will be ionized at neutral pH because all hydroxyl groups behave as strong acids in water.
D. It will dissolve identically to glucose because both molecules contain at least one oxygen atom.

11. A student compares sodium chloride in water with sodium chloride in a much less polar solvent. Which analysis best explains the likely difference in ionic interaction strength?

A. Ionic interactions are weaker in water because the higher dielectric constant reduces electrostatic attraction.
B. Ionic interactions are weaker in the less polar solvent because lower dielectric constants favor charge separation.
C. Ionic interactions are identical in both solvents because the ionic charges do not depend on the medium.
D. Ionic interactions vanish in water because hydrogen bonding eliminates all Coulombic attraction between ions.

12. A molecule contains both an ester linkage and a carboxyl group. After hydrolysis of the ester, which new product pair is most likely generated from that linkage?

A. An alcohol and a carboxylic acid.
B. A ketone and an alcohol.
C. An aldehyde and an ether.
D. A phenol and a hydrocarbon.

13. A biochemical reaction has an equilibrium constant much greater than 1, but the measured concentration of product remains low in the cell. Which explanation best accounts for this observation?

A. The reaction must have a positive delta G degree prime despite the large equilibrium constant.
B. The cell may continuously remove product through a downstream pathway, preventing its accumulation.
C. The reaction cannot occur in vivo because equilibrium constants apply only to purified laboratory systems.
D. The product concentration must eventually equal the reactant concentration when the enzyme becomes saturated.

14. A side chain in a protein active site has a pKa near physiological pH. Why is this especially important for catalysis?

A. The side chain is likely unable to change protonation state and therefore remains chemically inert.
B. The side chain can shift between protonated and deprotonated forms and participate in acid-base catalysis.
C. The side chain is permanently negatively charged and can only stabilize positively charged substrates.
D. The side chain must be located in a hydrophobic pocket where pH has no influence on reactivity.

15. A patient hyperventilates during an anxiety episode, causing rapid loss of carbon dioxide. Which interpretation best connects Chapter 1 chemistry to the likely physiological effect?

A. Loss of carbon dioxide can shift buffer equilibria and raise blood pH, altering protonation of biomolecules.
B. Loss of carbon dioxide can increase blood entropy and therefore lower the activation energies of most enzymes.
C. Loss of carbon dioxide can directly hydrolyze ATP and thereby force the bicarbonate system toward acidity.
D. Loss of carbon dioxide can eliminate hydrogen bonding in plasma and therefore denature all blood proteins.

## Vignette 
At intestinal pH 5.5, approximately what percentage of salicylic acid in the small intestine is present in the more water‑soluble (ionized) form A⁻?
A. About 9%  
B. About 50%  
C. About 91%  
D. About 99%

Even though salicylic acid is mostly ionized and more water‑soluble in the small intestine at pH 5.5, it is still significantly absorbed there. Which statement best explains this?
A. The ionized, water‑soluble form crosses membranes more efficiently than the unionized, lipid‑soluble form.  
B. The small intestine has a large surface area and long transit time, so even a modest unionized fraction can drive substantial absorption.  
C. Because intestinal pH equals the pKa of salicylic acid, it is 50% unionized and therefore absorbed maximally.  
D. Water‑solubility alone guarantees high absorption, independent of unionized fraction or membrane permeability.

## Instructor notes

These questions are intended to test application, analysis, and evaluation rather than recall alone, and several items require students to distinguish closely related ideas such as spontaneity versus rate or polarity versus ionization [file:111][file:122]. The small calculation set includes free-energy addition, simple rate-law reasoning, and pH-pKa interpretation because those were emphasized in the Chapter 1 activities and clicker prompts [file:111][file:122].
