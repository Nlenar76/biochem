## Zymogens Defined
Zymogens are an inactive form of enzymes also known as proenzymes and are how organisms protect themselves from their own digestive enzymes
May require modification for activation
Examples
- trypsinogen
- pepsinogen
> **Notes:** Upon cleavage, trypsinogen forms trypsin and pepsinogen is converted into pepsin

## Pancreatic Enzymes Defined
Pancreatic enzymes are proteases that cleave peptide (amide) linkages between amino acids
Examples
  - trypsin
  - pepsin
> **Notes:** trypsin and pepsin are examples of serine proteases that have (Asp-His-Ser) in their catalytic triad
## Peptidases Defined
Peptidases are enzymes that clave peptides into smaller individual amino acids or small peptides (di- or tripeptides)
Examples
  - aminopeptidase
  - carboxypeptidase
  - transmembrane peptidases
> **Notes:** Transmembrane peptidases are integral membrane proteins that break dipeptides and tripeptides into free amino acids; most peptides longer than three amino acids are not absorbed

## Transamination Defined
Transamination is the process in which ammonia can be removed from an amino acid.
The amine is transferred to an α-keto acid.
Produces a new amino acid and a new α-keto acid.
May use corresponding transaminases
  - e.g., alanine transaminase (ALT)
> **Notes:** ɑ keto acids can include pyruvate and ɑ-ketoglutarate
## Alanine Transaminase
Enzyme that transaminates pyruvate to the amino acid alanine
Occurs in the muscle
Alanine then travels from the muscle to the liver, where it can be transaminated to glutamate from α-ketoglutarate
> **Notes:** Pyruvate undergoes gluconeogenesis
## Oxidative Deamination Defined
Oxidative deamination is the process in which the amine is removed from ammonia.
Also involves an α-keto acid
NADH/H+ is generated.
Glutamate is commonly used and interconverted to α-ketoglutarate.
Free ammonia may be converted to waste products.
> **Notes:** Urea (mammalian waste product) can be produced
## Glutamate Dehydrogenase
Enzyme used to oxidatively deaminate glutamate and regenerate α-ketoglutarate
Located in the mitochondrial matrix of the kidney, the liver, and nervous tissue
Regulated allosterically by ADP and NAD+
Can lead to ammonia toxicity because this is a reversible reaction
> **Notes:** If ammonia levels are high (100uM), this can lead to depletion of mitochondrial a-ketoglutarate and disturbances of the citric acid cycle and ATP production.
## Glucose–Alanine Shuttle
Metabolic process through which nitrogenous wastes generated in muscle are transferred to pyruvate, generating alanine; the alanine in turn is transported to the liver, where it is deaminated and the pyruvate presumably used in gluconeogenesis.
Can deliver glucose to tissues in need OR amines to the liver for detoxification

## Glutamine Synthetase
ATP-dependent enzyme responsible for the synthesis of glutamine from glutamate and ammonium ion in the liver
Regulated via covalent modification (adenylation) and allosteric regulation
Allosteric regulators include
  - Carbamoyl phosphate
  - AMP, CTP
  - Ala, Gly, Ser, His, and Trp
> **Notes:** Found in the liver
## Glutaminase
Main enzyme that deaminates glutamine and yields glutamate and free ammonia
May be found in high levels in a high-fat diet

## The Urea Cycle Defined
The metabolic pathway that generates urea from carbamoyl phosphate
Function is to detoxify and dispose of ammonia
Occurs in two subcellular compartments
  - Mitochondrial matrix (first two reactions)
  - Cytosol (remaining steps)
## Carbamoyl Phosphate
Compound created from ammonia in order to reduce ammonia levels in the blood and lower the risk for toxicity
Produced in three stages
  - $CO_2$ reacts with ATP to form a carboxyl phosphate.
  - The phosphate is exchanged for a molecule of ammonia, creating a carbamate.
  - The carbamate is phosphorylated by a second molecule of ATP to produce carbamoyl phosphate.

## Carbamoyl Phosphate Synthetase
Enzyme used to produce carbamoyl phosphate
Has two isoforms
  - Carbamoyl phosphate synthetase I (CPS-1—mitochondrial form) involved in the production of carbamoyl phosphate for the urea cycle
  - Carbamoyl phosphate synthetase II (CPS-2—cytosolic form) responsible for the production of building blocks for pyrimidine biosynthesis
## Nitrogen Metabolism Regulation
Can occur enzymatically through CPS-1 (allosteric) or other enzymes in the pathway (substrate availability)
CPS-1 is allosterically activated by N-Acetylglutamate (NAG).

## Ammonotelic versus Uricotelic Organisms
Ammonotelic
- Typically eliminate wastes as ammonia secreted through the skin
- Include: Insects, crustaceans, aquatic invertebrates, and some fish and amphibians
Uricotelic
- Synthesize uric acid and eliminate it in their waste, which is technically not urine
- Include birds and reptiles

## Xenobiotics Defined
Xenobiotics are
  - Molecules the body recognizes as foreign
  - Not molecules found in human metabolism and need to be eliminated
  - May be cleaved into smaller fragments
## Xenobiotic Metabolism Defined
Xenobiotic metabolism is the process by which the body detoxifies and eliminates foreign molecules.
Xenobiotic metabolism creates molecules that are more water-soluble.
Can be divided into two phases
  - Phase I
  - Phase II
> **Notes:** This decreases the likelihood of xenobiotics entering a cell through the plasma membrane, and increases the likelihood of their being eliminated in the urine
## Phases of Xenobiotic Metabolism 
Phase I
- Oxidation or cleavage occurs
- Xenobiotics become more hydrophilic
- Products are metabolically active
Phase II
- Conjugation to larger molecules such as glutathione or glucuronic acid

## Cytochrome P450 Enzymes Defined
Cytochrome P450 enzymes are
  - Broad family of enzymes that employ NADPH and molecular oxygen (O2) to oxidatively modify substrate molecules
  - Membrane-associated enzymes commonly found in either the inner mitochondrial membrane or the endoplasmic reticulum
> **Notes:** Abbreviated as CYP
> globular, predominantly α helical proteins
## Monoamine Oxidases Defined
Monoamine oxidases
  - Are enzymes that deactivate neurotransmitters
  - Covalently fix FAD+ to catalyze the conversion of a primary amine to an aldehyde
  - Are found in two isoforms (MAO-A and MAO-B)
> **Notes:** Abbreviated as MAO
> MAO-I (inhibitors) are a class of antidepressants

## Glomerulus Defined
Glomerulus is a network of highly porous capillaries that act as filters.
Large objects are retained in the capillaries, and small molecules are filtered out.
> **Notes:** Bowman’s capsule – a glove that envelops the glomerulus and collects the filtrate

## Antidiuretic Hormone Defined
Antidiuretic hormone is a peptide hormone that acts via a seven-transmembrane helix spanning receptor to elevate cytosolic levels of cAMP.
  - Elevated cAMP concentrations activate protein kinase A
  - Proteins on the surface of vesicles containing aquaporins are phosphorylated.
  - This facilitates water transport.
