## Fatty Acids
Amphipathic, unbranched long-chain carboxylic acids, containing an even number of carbon atoms that are components of neutral lipids, phospholipids, and eicosanoids
Stored as triacylglycerols in adipose (fat) tissue
Serve to insulate and cushion vital organs
Generally found as esters and amides
> **Notes:** Amphipathic = contain both polar and nonpolar characteristics
> Generally between 12 – 26 carbons (most are between 16 and 18C)
> Explain free fatty acids (fatty acids not a part of an ester or an amide)
## Classification of Fatty Acids
Saturated
Do not contain double bonds
Unsaturated
Contain double bonds
Monounsaturated (has one double bond)
Polyunsaturated (has multiple double bonds)
> **Notes:** All double bonds are cis

## Neutral Lipids Defined
The classification of fatty acids that do not have charged groups because the carboxylic acid is esterified to either glycerol or cholesterol
Can form
  - monoacylglycerol (one fatty acid)
  - diacylglycerol (two fatty acids)
  - triacylglycerol (three fatty acids)
> **Notes:** These molecules are also referred to as a monoglyceride, diglyceride or triglyceride, respectively
> Can be categorized as fats /oils based on melting points

## Beta-Oxidation Defined
The breakdown of fatty acids
  - Each cycle shortens the fatty acid by 2C.
  - Acetyl-CoA, NADH, and $FADH_2$ are the final products.
Occurs in mitochondria of nearly every cell
Activation occurs through coupling to Coenzyme A to form acyl-CoA
> **Notes:** Activation for any molecule is achieved by coupling it to another molecule, which results in a product with different properties than the original.
> Most importantly - leads to the controlled harvesting of electrons that are used in oxidative phosphorylation to generate a proton gradient across the inner mitochondrial membrane, ultimately resulting in the generation of ATP.

## Beta-Oxidation of Unsaturated and Odd Numbered Fatty Acids
Unsaturated fatty acids
Trans double bonds are bonds that are metabolized differently.
Odd numbered fatty acids
Propionyl-CoA is formed, which is converted to succinyl-CoA.
> **Notes:** Succinyl-CoA enters the TCA cycle
## Fatty Acid Transport
Long-chain acyl-CoAs cannot penetrate the inner mitochondrial membrane
Fatty acids are shuttled across the inner mitochondrial membrane as acyl carnitines
Carnitine palmitoyl transferase (CPT1) catalyzes transesterification
Formation of acyl carnitine is the rate-limiting step of this process

## Section 9.3 Learning Objective

## Fatty Acid Biosynthesis
Requires the input of energy
Built from 2C units
Process is similar to beta-oxidation, but it is not the reverse.
Requires both acetyl-CoA carboxylase and fatty acid synthase
## Acetyl-CoA Carboxylase
Enzyme that synthesizes malonyl-CoA and releases it into the cytosol
Found in the endoplasmic reticulum
Rate-limiting step of fatty acid biosynthesis is catalyzed by this enzyme.
> **Notes:** Malonyl-CoA is a substrate of fatty acid synthase (FAS)
